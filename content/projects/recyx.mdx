## The problem

RECYX formalizes peer-to-peer exchanges of recyclable commodities. The difficult part was not the storefront; it was protecting inventory and account balances when multiple buyers tried to claim the same material at nearly the same time.

## Defending the invariant

The FastAPI transaction route uses SQLAlchemy and PostgreSQL at `READ COMMITTED` isolation. It locks the target commodity row with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, validates the available balance and item state, writes the debit and credit pair, and changes the commodity to `CLAIMED` inside one short transaction.

<Decision title="Pessimistic locking over optimistic retries">
  Contention is concentrated on a single listing during a claim. Row-level
  locking makes the database defend the invariant directly; competing requests
  wait, then receive a clean conflict instead of racing through stale reads. The
  trade-off is a lock held for the transaction, so external calls stay outside
  that boundary.
</Decision>

## Retry-safe transactions

Client submissions receive a unique transaction hash, making retries idempotent and preventing a dropped connection from creating a second balance deduction or reward.

## Evidence

A pytest-asyncio test fired 50 requests at one commodity: one claim succeeded, 49 returned `409 Conflict`, and the ledger remained balanced. Critical financial and checkout routes reached more than 92% test coverage across pytest and Jest; local benchmarks averaged under 60 ms for transaction confirmation at 50 concurrent requests.
