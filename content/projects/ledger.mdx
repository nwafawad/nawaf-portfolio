## The premise

Dense operations interfaces often make every row look equally urgent. Ledger explores a review experience where exceptions carry their own context and the queue explains why an item deserves attention.

## The design question

How might a reviewer move quickly without losing the evidence needed to make a defensible decision?

<ProjectArtifact variant="ledger" caption="A queue concept balancing volume, confidence, and exception states." />

## A layered information model

The interface separates triage from investigation. Reviewers can understand risk and confidence at the queue level, then expand only the evidence relevant to the selected exception.

<Decision title="Explain priority">
The queue exposes the reason for escalation instead of relying on an unexplained score.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Keep the audit trail close">
Supporting evidence sits beside the decision state, reducing context switching during review.
</Decision>

## What this study demonstrates

Ledger is concept work, not a shipped finance product. It demonstrates hierarchy for dense data, progressive disclosure, explainable system states, and interface consistency across a high-attention workflow.
