## The premise

The fastest route is not always the most useful one. Wayfinder explores how a journey planner might account for transfers, walking effort, service confidence, and the user&apos;s tolerance for uncertainty.

## The design question

How might route choices communicate lived effort as clearly as they communicate arrival time?

<ProjectArtifact variant="wayfinder" caption="A route concept showing time, change points, and confidence together." />

## Compare the whole journey

Each option is framed as a set of understandable tradeoffs. Time remains visible, but it sits beside the number of changes, walking distance, and confidence in the connection.

<Decision title="Make uncertainty visible">
Connection risk is communicated before selection, when it can still change the user&apos;s decision.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Design for scanning under pressure">
The route card uses plain language and a consistent rhythm so differences remain legible on the move.
</Decision>

## What this study demonstrates

Wayfinder is concept work, not a live transport service. It demonstrates service framing, mobile hierarchy, uncertainty communication, and inclusive decision support.
